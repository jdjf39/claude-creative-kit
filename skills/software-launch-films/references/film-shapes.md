# Film shapes

Pick ONE shape per film. Each has a skeleton at ~30 s, the play device it suits and the references it comes from. Times are starting values; move them to the music grid.

## 1. Collaborators (Comfy Agent)
Two named characters work the product together: the human ("YOU") and the product's agent (its name). Their cursors move independently with name-tag pills, and the agent does the hard part while the human only points or approves.
- 0–3 s: dark canvas, the human's cursor with its tag drops the trigger (a file, a request, a call). The agent's tag enters from off-frame, oversized, and lands.
- 3–12 s: the agent works the case across the UI (wires, scores, flags, writes); each action is a visible cursor action with a sound. A light statement frame interrupts once ("It hears every call.").
- 12–20 s: the payoff: the agent finishes something the human could not (a ranked result, a fix). Huge bold words over the result ("SCORE. COACH. CLOSE.").
- 20–27 s: proof montage of 2 to 3 more cases at speed, both tags visible.
- 27–30 s: the agent tag becomes the logo pill, wordmark, one line.
Best for: AI assistants, copilots, any "it does the work with you" product.

## 2. Overnight (Orthogonal Dots)
A request before bed, the product works while the person sleeps, they wake to the result.
- 0–4 s: evening, one request typed. 4–6 s: lights off, the person leaves the frame.
- 6–20 s: the product's agents (creatures or cursors) work: lists spin, cards fan out, tables fill, a clock in the corner jumps hours.
- 20–26 s: morning light, "Your pipeline is ready." The result card.
- 26–30 s: lockup.
Best for: async agents, batch processing, "review every call overnight".

## 3. Versus (Treg)
The product against the old way or a competitor, as a fight.
- 0–3 s: the claim line with the opponent named inline.
- 3–6 s: VS split screen (diagonal seam, two tiles, prices or times under each).
- 6–20 s: three rounds, each a race (bars, timers, counters) the product wins; WINNER sash on each win; one feature line with its live proof per round.
- 20–27 s: the product's best feature shown live, no opponent.
- 27–30 s: lockup with the install line or URL.
Best for: challenger products, "10x faster/cheaper than X". Never invent numbers: races must use real or clearly demo data.

## 4. Keystroke (One Inbox)
A physical keycap pressed on screen triggers each beat; the UI answers.
- Each beat: keycap enters (oversized, settles), press squash on the beat, the UI responds within 2 frames, a 3-to-5-word line ("Answer in a keystroke.").
- Alternate light frames (keycaps, lines) and dark frames (the UI).
Best for: keyboard-first and speed products.

## 5. One continuous world (Claude, Gemini, Citation Outreach)
No cuts at all: the camera flies through one big UI surface, magnifying the part that acts, pulling back to show where it lives, moving to the next.
- Needs the play added separately (a character cursor, an accent tag that travels) or it reads calm and corporate.
Best for: products whose UI is beautiful and spatial.

## 6. Missed-opportunity cascade (Citation Outreach)
The problem shown in the product's own vocabulary: windows or rows pile up with warning tags, a red pill lands ("Opportunity Missed!"), then the product reverses each one.
- 0–8 s: the pile-up. 8–10 s: the red pill. 10–25 s: the product turns each red item green, one by one, accelerating. 25–30 s: lockup.
Best for: products that catch what humans miss.

## 7. Storybook world (Jog)
The product's world drawn as an illustrated place with mascot characters; the UI appears as a sign, an easel, a letter inside that world.
Best for: brands with warmth and humour; needs strong illustration (or a style the user approved).

## 8. Before / After split
The same task, left the old way (grey, slow, frustrated cursor), right with the product (accent, fast). Both clocks run; the right side finishes first and takes the whole frame.
Best for: workflow replacement. Proven for Acme (the "split" ad).

## 9. Notification storm
The product's alerts pile in faster and faster until the frame is full, then one action clears them all.
Best for: monitoring, inboxes, coaching alerts. Proven for Acme (the "storm" ad, his favourite of that batch).

## Combining
A film can borrow one beat from a second shape (a Versus race inside a Collaborators film) but keeps one play device and one spine.
